## The problem

A small clothing store was building a marketing plan without knowing who its customers were. The decision was which segment to focus on.

## The data

The data contained 2,000 customer records with gender, marital status, age, education, income, occupation and settlement size. It was 54.3% male and 45.7% female; 50.3% of customers were single. Most customers were aged 25–40.

Education and occupation added useful context: 69.3% finished high school, 14.6% were undergraduates and 1.8% attended graduate school. Skilled employees or officials represented 55.7%, unemployed or unskilled customers 31.7% and managers 12.7%.

## The approach

The elbow method was used to choose the cluster count. The curve flattened past 4, and 3 was chosen. Two clustering methods then ran independently: K-means++ and agglomerative clustering.

Both methods produced the same three groups with different labels. That agreement is the key technical finding: the segmentation is a property of the customers, not of the algorithm.

The recommendation is to focus on the mid segment, then the lower. Both are price-sensitive and promotion-responsive, most did not study past high school, and both sit under $130k income. The range should be casual, comfortable and affordable. With gender split near 50/50, keep the range balanced or lean unisex.

## What I would do differently

- I would keep both clustering methods as a cross-check rather than treating one algorithm's labels as the answer.
- I would use the segment profiles to keep the marketing recommendation connected to the customer data.
